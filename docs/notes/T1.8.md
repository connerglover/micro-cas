## Public API as implemented

Levels are in brackets. Every function from §21.2 is present. Extensions are optional
arguments, so callers written to the frozen API still work.

- **`postorder(e)` [7]:** the ascending list of reachable ids. Ids are topological, so children
  come first. Preallocated ids are included. `e` may also be a flat tuple of roots. It uses a
  bytearray mark-and-scan: no sort, no recursion.
- **`rebuild(n, new)` [4]:** node `n` with each child `c` replaced by `new.get(c, c)`, rebuilt
  through the constructors. It returns `n` itself when no child changed, so it accepts both a
  changed-only map and the "every node" map of the `g_rewrite` sketch (§9.7).
  - A CALL keeps its head, which is a name.
  - A changed HOLD folds to its value.
  - Relations go through `rel_`, so they may fold to TRUE or FALSE.
  - **T1.9 can call `A.rebuild` instead of writing its own** (§17.1 lists `rebuild` under
    CVRULE). This saves device code.
- **`subst(e, x, v)` [3]:** replaces node `x` by `v`. `x` and `v` may be equal-length tuples,
  which gives a simultaneous substitution (a swap works). `x` may be any node, including a
  kernel such as `sin(x)`. Symbol masks skip untouched subtrees. Constructor errors propagate:
  `Undefined` for `1/0`, and `1/oo` gives 0.
- **`linear(e, x)` [5]** gives `(a, b)`; **`quadratic(e, x)` [5]** gives `(a, b, c)`. Both
  return None when `e` is not of that form.
  - **`a` is ZERO when `e` does not depend on it** (giac `is_linear_wrt` semantics). The LIN
    and QUAD wildcards must therefore test `a != K.ZERO` themselves (§9.2).
  - The match is structural, on the canonical form. It goes through nested sums and products
    (`3y(2x+1)` gives `(6y, 3y)`, `(2x-3)^2` gives `(4, -12, 9)`). Both use the shared core
    `pco(e, x, n)` [6].
- **`split_const(e, x)` [5]:** `(c, g)` with `e = c*g`.
  - `c` holds the numeric coefficient and the factors that are free of x.
  - `g` holds the factors that depend on x, with coefficient 1.
  - It gives `(e, ONE)` when `e` is free of x, and `(ONE, e)` when `e` is not a product.
  - `mul_(ONE, [(c, ONE), (g, ONE)]) == e` holds exactly; a property test checks it.
- **`degree(e, x)` [6]:** the polynomial degree in `x`. It is 0 when `e` is free of x, and -1
  when `e` is not a polynomial (negative or symbolic powers, a function of x). `x` may be a
  kernel.
- **`size(e, extra=False)` [6]:** the printed-tree size, counted with multiplicity.
  - A leaf counts 1.
  - A sum counts 1, plus 1 for a nonzero constant, plus each term, plus 1 for each coefficient
    other than 1.
  - A product counts 1, plus 1 for a coefficient other than 1, plus each base, plus each
    exponent other than 1.
  - Any other node counts 1 plus its children.
  - `extra=True` gives the §8.7 measure: plus 2 per numeric radical pair and plus 1 per
    negative numeric exponent.
- **`simplify_candidate(e)` [7]:** true for any of these three:
  - a sum together with a negative numeric power;
  - a `tan`, `sec`, `csc` or `cot`;
  - a numeric radical other than `sqrt(integer)` (FlowCE's rule: cube roots, `2^(3/4)`,
    Gaussian radicands).

  It accepts any result: lists, relations, sentinels, CALL and HOLD (HOLD's integer powers are
  not radicals).
- **`nprog(e, xs)` [4]:** the flat program of §6.6, with the root last.
  - Numbers used as coefficients and exponents are folded into the parent instruction.
  - An exponent becomes an int, or a float `p/q` with the flag `s = ±1.0` when `q` is odd.
    Other exponents get `P_POW`.
  - It raises `Undefined` for oo, undef, relations, lists, CALL, BIGO and sentinels, and for a
    symbol that is not in `xs`.
  - HOLD aliases its product.
  - Big rationals are converted by `qf` [7] (scaled to 64 bits, `ldexp`), never by
    `float(big int)` (device-python §5.1).
- **`nrun(prog, vals, r=None)` [4]:** returns a float, or a complex when the value is not
  real. An exactly zero imaginary part is demoted to a float.
  - **It raises only `Undefined`.** It converts ZeroDivisionError, OverflowError and
    ValueError itself, and it checks every value for inf and nan.
  - The optional `r` receives every value. Use it to reuse the buffer, as quadrature should,
    and to feed `ntol`.
  - It resets `LEFTREAL[0]` at the start.
- **`nev(e, env=None)` [3]:** `env` maps SYM ids to numbers.
- **`ntol(prog, r, t)` [4]:** the absolute tolerance of the last run (see the probe below).
  It returns -1.0 for a point that says nothing.
- **`nprobe(a, b=None)` [3]:** compares `a` with `b`, or with 0 when `b` is None. It returns
  `NO` (0), `LIKELY` (1) or `UNKNOWN` (-1). It builds no nodes.
- **Constants and helpers:**
  - constants: `P_CONST P_VAR P_ADD P_MUL P_POW P_FN`, `TOL = 1e-9`, `LEFTREAL`;
  - tables: `FNUM` (`#@table 7`, takes a float) and `FCPX` (`#@table 5`, takes a complex),
    indexed by `op - FN0`;
  - helpers: `cpow` [6], `clog cexp csqrt nabs` [7], the complex trig and hyperbolic functions,
    `fsinh fcosh ftanh` (absent from the device `math`), and `ffact` (Gamma by Lanczos, exact
    products for integers).

## Deviations from DESIGN.md, with reasons

1. **`LEFTREAL` is per operation.** It is set when an operation with real operands gives a
   non-real value, such as `sqrt` of a negative, `ln` of a negative, `asin(2)` or a
   non-integer power of a negative. A complex constant does not set it: `(x+i)^2` is evaluated
   and compared. This is the "every subexpression real" rule of §8.3, and it removes the
   design's vague "every input was real".
2. **The probe's tolerance is propagated (`ntol`).** §8.6 uses `1e-9 * (largest term or 1)`
   instead.
   - Each leaf carries `TOL` relative. The tolerance is an exact upper bound through sums and
     products, and uses the derivative through powers and functions.
   - The spec's scale fails both ways:
     - It gives a false NO for `10^20*(sin²+cos²-1)` and for `sqrt(sin²+cos²-1)`.
     - It calls `x/10^12` zero.
   - A point is unusable (-1) in these cases:
     - a divisor, a log argument or a symbolic base is within its tolerance of 0;
     - an argument is within its tolerance of a jump (floor, ceil, sign) or of the `asin`
       branch point;
     - a function or power value has a tolerance above `1e-3` of its size, such as `sec` of a
       huge, imprecise argument.

     Without this, random nonzero expressions such as `-sec(e^20.3)` or `tan(cosh(20)/x)`
     came out LIKELY.
   - Measured on 5,500 random identities (`(u+v)^2`, `sin²+cos²`, `sin(u+v)`, `sinh`,
     `cosh²-sinh²`, `ln(3u²)`, ...): 0 false NO. On about 3,900 random nonzero trees: 0 false
     LIKELY. Both properties are tests.
3. **The probe returns `UNKNOWN`** when no point is usable after 8 tries. This is the "no real
   point" case of §8.7. Point schedule: positive, positive, negative, then alternating. A
   seeded integer LCG (`s = (75s+74) mod 65537`, seed 4321) gives each symbol its own value in
   `(0.31, 2.7)`. Symbols are taken in name order, so `nprobe(a, b)` is deterministic and the
   same on every platform. If `b` fails where `a` is fine (Undefined, or `b` cannot be
   compiled), the result is NO.
4. **Complex branch conventions** follow SymPy, giac and Mathematica. They differ from
   CPython's `cmath` on the cuts:
   - `asin(2) = π/2 - i·ln(2+√3)`, and `asin` and `acos` are odd on the real cuts, which
     matches T1.1's D15 `asin(-u) = -asin(u)`;
   - `atan(z) = (i/2)(ln(1-iz) - ln(1+iz))`;
   - `ln` is principal, with -0.0 read as +0.0;
   - `casin` evaluates in the half-plane where `iz + sqrt(1-z²)` does not cancel, then uses
     oddness, so `asin(-1e10)` is accurate.
5. **`size`, the `simplify_candidate` radical rule and `degree`'s -1** are defined here,
   because §8.7 and §21.2 do not define them precisely.
6. **Depth.** The decimal-line chain is now 7, not 5 (§17.2): `PR.record` 2 > `nev` 3 >
   `nrun` 4 > `cacos` 5 > `casin` 6 > `clog` 7. This is still within the budget. The deepest
   `alg` chain is 7.

## Known gaps

- `linear`, `quadratic` and `degree` are structural, not derivative-based like giac.
  - `(x+1)^2 - x^2` is not seen as linear.
  - They return None when a coefficient would be the product of two non-numeric factors:
    `y*(x+z)` (because of `y*z`), `(x+y)^2`. `pco` is L6 and cannot call `mul_`. Expand
    first; after `expand` both work.
  - `x` should be a SYM or a function node. A sum or product used as "the variable" is not
    found inside flattened sums and products.
- The probe gives every symbol the same sign at a point (as designed), so mixed-sign domains
  are never sampled. It has no complex-point fallback: an identity that is nowhere real gives
  UNKNOWN.
- `ntol`'s derivative factors are bounds, not exact, for the complex arguments of `atan` and
  `tanh` near their poles, and for `ffact`.

## For downstream tasks

- **T1.9 (rules):**
  - `check` for LIN: `r = A.linear(s, x)`; it matches if `r is not None and r[0] != K.ZERO`.
    QUAD is the same with `A.quadratic`.
  - The numeric part of `guard_ok` (§9.2 step 2) is these steps:
    1. `p = A.nprog(d, ())`, then `r = [0.0]*len(p)` and `t = [0.0]*len(p)`.
    2. `v = A.nrun(p, (), r)`; an `Undefined` fails the guard.
    3. `w = A.ntol(p, r, t)`.
    4. If `w < 0`, the guard is undecided and fails. If `abs(v) > w`, decide by the sign of a
       real `v`; a complex `v` fails the guard. Otherwise return `(NEAR, op, d)`.
  - `A.rebuild(m, new)` is available for `g_rewrite`.
- **T1.10 (norm):**
  - `g_iszero` step 5 is `A.nprobe(e)`. NO means NO. LIKELY means LIKELY. For UNKNOWN (no
    usable real point), decide in `g_iszero`. LIKELY means "no counterexample found";
    returning NO would reject complex-valued correct results.
  - The simplify verification (§8.7) is `A.nprobe(A_form, W)`:
    - NO: discard W and do `SIMPBUG += 1`;
    - UNKNOWN: discard without counting a bug;
    - LIKELY: accept.
  - The measure is `A.size(e, True)`.
  - `A.simplify_candidate` is ready for `g_autosimp`.
- **T2.7 (quadrature):** call `nprog` once. Then call `nrun(prog, (x,), r)` per node with a
  reused `r`. `Undefined` marks a node to move inward.
- **M0.1 (invariants):** the rule-9 check (`abs(u)^even`) also fires on `abs(ln5-ln6)^4`.
  There `isreal` cannot prove `ln5-ln6` real, so `mul_` rightly keeps the `abs`. This is the
  T1.1 note's class of cases. `test_probe_never_says_no_to_an_identity` uses
  `invariants_skip('mul')` because its random inputs can build that.

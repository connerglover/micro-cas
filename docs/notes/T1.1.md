**Status.** 142 tests pass, in default and `--strict-depth` modes; lint and FRAMELINT are clean.
The golden dumps are byte-identical under `PYTHONHASHSEED` 0, 1 and 12345. Ad-hoc sweeps of about

## Public API as implemented (§21.2 frozen)

Levels in brackets.

- **Op codes and ids** as in §5.2/§5.5. Two more op codes, `GT = 20` and `GE = 21`, are accepted
  **only by `rel_`**, which swaps them into `LT`/`LE`. They are never stored. `DEAD = 7` marks a
  released slot under the debug arena. `ODDF` and `EVENF` are tuples of op codes.
- **Table:** `N H NX V B NN NMAX(768) BASE SYMN SYMI RULECACHE TCACHE FCACHE DEBUG_ARENA`.
  Hooks: `ESC`, `MEMFREE`, `ONRESET` (`#@native`; conftest fills them).
- **Exceptions:** `CasError(msg)`, `CasSoft`, `Undefined(CasSoft)`, `Cancel(BaseException)`,
  `CasMemory(BaseException)`. Message constants: `M_COMPLEX`, `M_BIG`, `M_RESULT`.
- **Intern and regions.**
  - `mk(t)` [7]: hash-conses, preallocated slots. Raises `CasError('expression too complex')` at
    `NMAX`. Every 256 ids it checks `MEMFREE() < LOWMEM`, calls `gc.collect()`, then raises
    `CasMemory`.
  - `release(m)` [7].
  - `keep(m, y)` [4]: `y` is `None`, an id or a flat tuple. `None` inside the tuple is allowed
    and passed through.
  - `reset()` [4]: calls `ONRESET` first, then clears `SYMN`, `SYMI`, `RULECACHE`, `TCACHE` and
    `FCACHE`.
- **Kernels** [7] on `(NUM, a, b, d)` and `(FLT, re, im)` tuples:
  - `nnorm(a, b, d)`, `nadd`, `nmul`, `ninv`, `nneg`, `nsign`, `ncmp`, `npowi(x, n)` (MAXBITS
    guard).
  - `fpow(x, y)`: a FLT power with the real-odd-root rule.
  - FLT contagion lives in the kernels. Overflow, inf and nan raise `CasError('number too
    large')`; a zero divisor raises `Undefined`.
- **Factoring:** `ifac(n)` [7] gives `(p1, a1, ...)`. Trial division runs to `TRIAL = 10^4`; then
  the cofactor gets a perfect-power test (up to 4000 bits). Results are cached per command and
  the loops poll `ESC`. `rootsplit(n, s)` [6].
- **Predicates** [7]:
  - `negfirst(u)`, `isninf(u)`, `free_of(e, x)`, `tiefix(lst)`;
  - `csign(u)`: +1/-1 for a provably positive/negative constant;
  - **`isreal(u)`**: provably real. Symbols count as real (§8.3);
  - `chk(x)`: rejects released ids (AssertionError) and sentinels (`CasError('invalid use of a
    result')`).
- **Leaves** [6]: `num(a, b=0, d=1)`, `flt(re, im=0.0)` (non-finite raises `Undefined`; -0.0
  becomes 0.0), `sym(name)`.
- **Constructors:**
  - `add_(terms, k=None)` [6]: `k` is `None`, a number tuple or a number id;
  - `neg_(u)` [6] and `absn(u)` [6];
  - `mul_(k, pairs)` [5]: `k` is a number id or tuple; `pairs` is a list of `(base id, exponent
    id)`;
  - `fn_(op, a)` [5] and `rel_(op, a, b)` [5].

## What the constructors do (beyond the §8.2 list)

- **Radicals use a per-prime canonical form.** Every real rational base under a rational
  exponent is factored (`ifac`), and the exponents are summed **per prime**. Integer parts go to
  the coefficient. Primes with equal fractional exponents are multiplied into one base (rule 11).
  - So `4^(1/3) = 2^(2/3)`, `√2·√6 = 2√3`, `12^(1/4) = √2·3^(1/4)` and `2^(1/2)·2^(1/3) =
    2^(5/6)`, whatever the build order.
  - A cofactor with only prime factors above 10^4 is an atom (partly reduced, as §6.2 allows).
- **Negative numeric bases:**
  - an odd root is real (`(-8)^(1/3) = -2`);
  - an even root uses `(-a)^(p/s) = a^(p/s)·e^(iπp/s)`. The factor `e^(iπ·m/4)` folds to `±1`,
    `±i` or `(±1±i)/√2`, so `√-4 = 2i` and `(-4)^(1/4) = 1+i`. Otherwise it stays as the pair
    `(E, iπp/s)`: `(-1)^(1/6)` is `e^(iπ/6)`;
  - this keeps products exact under principal values: `(-1)^(1/6)·(-1)^(1/6) = e^(iπ/3)`, not the
    real cube root -1.
  - `exp(iπ) = -1` and `exp(iπ/2) = i` come from the same rule.
- **Sum bases under integer exponents are sign-normalized** with `negfirst` (D15 extended):
  `2(1-x)` is `-2(x-1)` and `(1-x)(x-1) = -(x-1)^2`. Without this, the design's own rule
  `-1·S → -S` makes products non-associative: `((-1)(y-1))·2x` and `(-1)(y-1)·2·x` were
  different nodes, and the canonicity property test caught it.
- **Rules 7 and 9** (`√(u²) = |u|`, `|u|^2 = u^2`) and **`ln(e^u) = u`** apply only when `u` has
  symbols or `isreal(u)`. For a complex constant they are false:
  `√((1+i√2)^2) = 1+i√2`, not `√3`.
- **`absn`** takes out the modulus of any coefficient whose modulus is rational: `|3ix| = 3|x|`,
  `|(3+4i)x| = 5|x|`, `|16i√2| = 16√2`. With an irrational modulus the coefficient stays inside:
  `abs((1+i)x)`.
  - It also takes out the rational content of a sum (`|2x+2| = 2|x+1|`) and applies the sign
    rule.
  - `abs(w)` is `w` when every exponent is even (`|x²y⁴| = x²y⁴`). A provably positive constant
    is returned as is.
- **`fn_` special values:**
  - trig at every multiple of π/120 that reduces to π/12, π/10, π/8 or π/5 (all six functions);
  - poles raise `Undefined`;
  - the values are **sums** with rational coefficients: `cos(5π/12)` is
    `ADD(0, √6, 1/4, √2, -1/4)`, not `MUL(1/4, √6-√2, 1)`;
  - inverse trig of every table value **and its negative**. `ASIN_H` and `ATAN_H` are signed
    (m in -60..60), because D15 normalization can hand over `√3-2 = -tan(π/12)`. They are
    generated, and `tests/test_special.py` regenerates and compares them;
  - `ln` of any Gaussian rational: `ln(-2) = ln 2 + iπ`, `ln(1+i) = ln(2)/2 + iπ/4`,
    `ln(1+2i) = ln(5)/2 + i·atan(2)`. Perfect powers are split (`ln 8 = 3 ln 2`,
    `ln 36 = 2 ln 6`);
  - `n!` for integers `0 <= n <= 1000`; a negative n raises `Undefined`;
  - sinh, cosh, tanh at 0; sign, floor, ceil of numbers; sign of a provably signed constant;
  - the infinity table of §6.3. `ln(-∞)` is `undef` (my choice: no real value).
- **Floats.**
  - `fn_` leaves FLT arguments unevaluated (`sin(0.5)`). Per §6.3 they never reach the
    constructors in normal flow: `exact()` comes first, and `approx()` (T1.5/T1.6) evaluates with
    `nev`.
  - `add_` keeps a FLT-zero constant as FLT `0.0`.
  - `mul_` returns exact `ZERO` for a zero coefficient.
- **`rel_`:** `∞` differences fold. `∞ - ∞` gives `UNDEF`.

## Deviations from DESIGN.md

1. **Size (the big one).** `node.py` is **1153 minified lines and 15.2 KB of `.mpy`** (mpy-cross
   1.29), which is **26.8 KB of RAM at FACTOR 1.8**. The §4.1 budget is 520 lines and 12 KB.
   `build/bundle.py` reports both overruns. (CVPARSE is over its budget too: 657 lines and
   15.8 KB.) Per-function `.mpy` bytes:

   | function | bytes |
   |---|---|
   | `mul_` | 2154 |
   | `fn_` | 1860 |
   | `absn` | 889 |
   | `keep` | 761 |
   | `add_` | 723 |
   | `npowi` | 580 |
   | `mk` | 576 |
   | `tiefix` | 515 |
   | the rest | about 4.6 KB |
   | data and constants | 3.6 KB |

   - The overrun comes from the listed scope (kernels with FLT paths, 13 `mul_` rules with
     radicals and ∞, the special-value tables and their inverses, `keep` with `RULECACHE`), not
     from dead code. The L7-leaf rule also forces the gcd normalization to be inlined in each
     kernel.
   - For T1.15 / §18.8, the candidates for a lazy part, should the budget not close:
     - the special-value machinery (`fn_` trig, inverse and complex-`ln` paths plus the tables,
       about 3 KB of `.mpy`);
     - the ∞ table.

     Both need a hook, because node is a leaf module. **Not done here; it needs a design
     decision.**
2. **The debug arena's `NMAX` raise is test-side.** `K.DEBUG_ARENA` changes `release`, `keep` and
   `mk` as §20.4 says. The fixture `arena` in `tests/test_node.py` grows `N`, `H`, `NX` and `V`
   to 8192 and sets `K.NMAX`. This keeps about 15 resident lines off the device. Other test
   files can use it with `from test_node import arena`.
3. **`RULECACHE` entry format.** `keep` treats `entry[0]` of every rule entry as the pattern id,
   which matches §9.4 `(lhs_id, rhs_str, guard_str)`, and rewrites the entry tuples in place.
   `WILD` leaves must be `(WILD, slot:int, cons:int)`, because the hash uses `t[1] << 4` and
   `t[-1]`.
4. **`fn_` has no general argument reduction.** `sin(9π/7)` stays as typed. Only multiples of
   π/120 that hit the tables are folded.
5. **Seed 2.2 `sqrt(3+2√2) = 1+√2`** is denesting. §6.2 puts it in `simplify` (T1.10). Here the
   test checks that the constructor keeps it and that the value is equal.

## Known gaps

- **Merging exponents of a symbol-free, non-numeric base of unknown sign** (rule 10, and rule 5
  distribution). An example is `(1-π)^(1/6)·(1-π)^(1/6)`, which becomes `(1-π)^(1/3)` and then
  takes the real-root convention, while the principal product does not. This is the §8.3 merge
  as written. It is exact for numbers (they go per prime) and for symbols (the policy). The
  oracle in `test_node.py` skips such trees (`TreeEval` raises `Skip`).
- Inverse trig of numbers outside its real domain uses `asin(-u) = -asin(u)` (D15). On the
  branch cuts this differs from CPython's `cmath` signed-zero convention. `nev` must use the same
  odd extension.
- `abs(z^r)` for a Gaussian base `z` stays `abs(...)`, for example `abs(i^(3/2))`.
- The §5.7 FLT hash puts the exponent at bits 17-24 and the mantissa at bits 0-19. They overlap,
  so `0.5` and `1.25` collide. This is harmless (`tiefix` and the id compare), but the design
  owner may want `(x & 255) << 20`. Changing it changes the platform contract and
  `invariants.ref_hash`.

- **T1.5 (builders):**

  - `log(u)` is `mul_(ONE, [(fn_(LN, u), ONE), (LN10, MONE)])`, `sqrt(u)` is
    `mul_(ONE, [(u, HALF)])` and `e^u` is `mul_(ONE, [(E, u)])`;

- **T1.7 (printer):**
  - table values are sums with fractional coefficients, so content factoring for display
    (`(√6-√2)/4`) is the printer's job;
  - expect `e^(i*pi/6)` pairs and `-2*(x-1)` for `2(1-x)`;
  - `ln` of a complex number gives `i*atan(...)` terms;
  - FLT leaves carry floats as typed (`0.5`).
- **T1.8 / T1.10:**
  - `isreal`, `csign`, `ifac`, `rootsplit` and `tiefix` are available;
  - every numeric base in a MUL is a positive integer with an exponent in (0, 1), or a non-real
    Gaussian, or is under a non-numeric or complex exponent;
  - `-∞` is `MUL(-1, OO, 1)`;
  - `x·∞` stays a product.
- **T1.6 / T1.13:** use the `arena` fixture (above); `keep(m, None)` with a non-empty
  `RULECACHE` keeps the rule patterns.
- **Determinism.** Every dict iteration in node feeds a sort before any `mk`, so node ids do not
  depend on dict order on the device either.

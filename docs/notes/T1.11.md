## Acceptance (§21.4)

- **alg §6 seeds 2.8.** `x^4+4 = (x^2-2x+2)(x^2+2x+2)` (Sophie Germain), `x^6-1` (cyclotomic),
  `6x^3-11x^2+6x-1 = (x-1)(2x-1)(3x-1)`, `x^2-y^2`, `2x^3-4x^2-6x = 2x(x-3)(x+1)`.
  - **`x^5-x+1`, no false claim.** The answer is `x^5-x+1` itself; no answer text ever says
    "irreducible". Internally the search is complete (`COMPLETE[0] == 1`): there is no rational
    root, and the Kronecker search for a quadratic factor over 3 points finishes.
- **Product check.** `g_mfac` multiplies its factors back (polyc) and compares the result with
  its input exactly. If they differ, `FACTBUG` is incremented and the input comes back
  unfactored. The tests assert `FACTBUG == 0` after every test, and an injected bug shows that
  the check catches it.
- **Budgets.** The searches are bounded:
  - `KBUD = 6000` Kronecker odometer steps per `ufac` call;
  - `MBUD = 2000` image-factor subsets per `g_mfac` call;
  - `CYMAX = 128`: the largest cyclotomic index tried;
  - `FKMAX = 64`: the largest Kronecker image, `prod(deg_i + 1)`;
  - `TDIV = 10^6`: the integer trial-division bound.

  When a budget runs out, the answer is the correct partial factorization and `COMPLETE` is 0.
  Every loop polls `K.ESC` (`Cancel`). Measured on the desktop, the worst inputs take at most
  0.03 s, about 3 s on the device at the design's 100× factor.

## Public API as implemented

Levels are in brackets.

### `factor.py` (part a; `R.MODS['fact']`)

- **`g_factor(args, ctx)` [2]**: the registry command `factor(e, [x])`.
  - `x` must be a symbol; the command sets `ctx[P.X_MV] = x` for the printer. Any other second
    argument raises `CasError('invalid argument')` (`M_ARG`).
  - A list or a relation is factored element by element: `factor(x^2-1=0)` gives
    `(x-1)*(x+1)=0`.
  - It sets `COMPLETE[0]`.
- **`g_fac(e, ap)` [2]**: one expression.
  - An exact real rational goes to `ifactor`.
  - An expression that contains a FLT (the inert path, F4) or a sentinel comes back unchanged.
  - Otherwise `NO.normal(e)` gives `k*N/D`; if `normal` gave up, `e` comes back.
  - N and D become polyc dicts over kernels (`terms`). Each is factored by
    `FB.g_mfac(P, nv)` and rebuilt (`mkpoly`), then `mul_(k', pairs)`. The constructors keep a
    product of sums, so no HOLD is needed for polynomials.
  - `ap` is the approx flag (§6.3). It makes every factor monic in its leading term, so
    `factor(x^2-0.25)` prints `(x-0.5)*(x+0.5)`, as §6.3 asks.
- **`ifactor(q)` [4]**: a rational `(NUM, a, 0, d)` becomes `HOLD(MUL(sign, p1, e1, ...))`,
  by trial division by 2, 3 and 6k±1 up to `TDIV`. The denominator's primes get negative
  exponents. It returns the NUM itself when that says nothing more: 0, ±1, a prime, `2/3`.
  A cofactor above `TDIV^2` stays one base and clears `COMPLETE`.
- **`terms(e, ks, out)` [4]** and **`mkpoly(F, ks, dv)` [4]**: node ↔ polynomial.
  - The kernels are symbols, functions, `CALL`s and the like.
  - A radical `u^(n/q)` becomes kernel `u^(1/q)` to the power n.
  - A power `b^(n*u)`, with n a positive integer, becomes kernel `b^u` to the power n. So
    `e^(2x)-2e^x+1` gives `(e^x-1)^2`.
  - Any other power is a kernel kept whole.
  - Gaussian or non-integer coefficients make `terms` refuse, and the normal form comes back.
- **State:** `COMPLETE = [1]` (1: every factor of the last command is proven irreducible over
  Q), `TDIV`, `M_ARG`.

### `factorb.py` (part b; `R.MODS['factb']`)

- **`ufac(f)` [3]**: f is a dense Z[x] list (nonzero). It returns
  `(c, [(g, m), ...], comp)` with `f = c*prod(g^m)`. Each g is primitive, has `lc > 0` and
  degree ≥ 1; `comp` means every g is proven irreducible.
  - It runs `poly.yun`, then a worklist. Each square-free part is split by the first method
    that applies:
    1. **Binomials:** `binom`.
    2. **Rational roots:** `polyb.ratroots`. When a divisor list is incomplete, the numeric
       real roots are rounded to `p/q` for each `q | lc` and tested with `pdivex`
       (T1.2 notes).
    3. **Kronecker (`kron`):** factors of degree 1 (only if the rational roots were
       incomplete), then 2 .. deg/2. The search runs on `g(X)`, `X = x^e`, first (e is the
       exponent gcd; a factor `h(X)` gives `h(x^e)`), then on g.
  - A part is proven irreducible when every Kronecker degree was searched to the end, when
    `binom` proves it (Capelli), or when `binom` produced it as a cyclotomic `Phi_d(al*x, be)`.
  - Every call resets `STEPS` (budget `KBUD`). Callable from L2 generators.
- **`binom(g)` [4]**: g is `a*x^n + b`, primitive with `a > 0`. It returns
  `(factors, proven)`:
  - For the largest `k | n` with `a = al^k` and `|b| = be^k`, it gives the homogenized
    cyclotomic factors `Phi_d(al*X, be)`, `X = x^(n/k)`, for `d | k` when `b < 0`, and for
    `d | 2k`, `d ∤ k` when `b > 0`. With `X = x` these are final (`proven == 2`).
  - Otherwise it tries Sophie Germain, `u^4 + 4v^4` (both forms).
  - `(None, 1)` means irreducible by Capelli's theorem. Its two conditions are exactly these
    two searches, for a primitive binomial.
  - `(None, 0)` means a needed k was over `CYMAX`; `ufac` then falls through to rational
    roots and Kronecker (`x^257-1` still gives `(x-1)*...`).
- **`kron(f, d)` [4]**: returns `(h, complete)`.
  - h is a factor with `1 <= deg h <= d`, or None.
  - The search uses d+1 sample points, chosen from 0, ±1, ±2, ... as those with the fewest
    divisors (complete divisor lists only).
  - An odometer runs over signed divisors, with the first point positive only. It
    interpolates by divided differences, which must stay integers, then checks `lc(h) | lc(f)`
    and `h(0) | f(0)`, then `pdivex`.
  - A sample point that is a root returns `x - x0` at once.
- **`nroots(f)` [4]**: the real roots of a square-free dense f, as ascending floats.
  - It works through the derivative chain: `f^(k)` is monotone between consecutive real roots
    of `f^(k+1)`, so no root is missed.
  - Each root is found by Illinois regula falsi inside a Cauchy bound.
  - It returns `[]` when the floats would overflow.
  - It is accurate to about 1e-15 relative (tested against SymPy `real_roots`). **For T1.12's
    numeric fallback.**
- **`g_mfac(P, nv)` [2]**: P is a nonzero polyc dict in nv variables. It returns
  `(a, b, [(F, m), ...], comp)` with `P = (a/b)*prod(F^m)` exactly. The steps:
  1. **The monomial content.**
  2. **A binomial `a*U^d + b*V^d` in several variables.** It is factored through
     `a*T^d + b` (ufac), mapping `T^i -> U^i V^(k-i)`. This is complete: a unimodular monomial
     change makes it univariate. Examples: `x^4y^4-z^4`, `x^2y^2-1`, `a^3b^3+8c^3`.
  3. **A homogeneous P in several variables.** It is dehomogenized in its last variable and
     rehomogenized at the end, so `x^8-y^8` becomes univariate.
  4. **One variable left:** ufac.
  5. **Several:** the Kronecker image `u = P(X^w0, X^w1, ...)`, with `prod(deg_i+1) <= FKMAX`.
     u is factored by ufac. Then subsets of its factors (with multiplicity), fewest first,
     are multiplied, mapped back (`munkron`) and tested with `mdivex`. A hit is a true factor,
     and it is irreducible when the image factorization was complete. This covers what §11.2
     calls "content per variable" (`a*x^2-a`, `x*y+x+y+1`) and also non-homogeneous cases
     (`x^2+2xy+y^2-1 = (x+y-1)(x+y+1)`).
  6. **The product check** (FACTBUG).
- **`iroot(n, k)` [7]**: the exact integer k-th root, or 0. **`cyclo(n)` [5]**:
  `{d: Phi_d}` for `d | n`.
- **State:** `KBUD MBUD CYMAX FKMAX FACTBUG STEPS`.

## Deviations from DESIGN.md, and why

1. **Aggregate size remains above the original estimate.** The family is 515 minified lines
   against the section 4.1 estimate of 280, but every compile unit now fits the provisional
   150-line limit. The existing total cap of 520 is retained. Splitting controls compile peak;
   it does not establish that the complete program fits the EVO heap. No new `.mpy` measurement
   was made; T1.14/T1.15 must measure the final bundle and device RAM.
2. **The integer content of a polynomial is not prime-factored.** §11.2 says trial division
   applies "for an integer (and the integer content of a polynomial)". I follow giac/FlowCE:
   `factor(12x^2-12)` gives `12*(x-1)*(x+1)`. A HOLD around polynomial factors would also
   break the round trip and `approx`.
3. **Kronecker searches every degree up to deg/2**, not only degree 2 (plus 3 from degree 6),
   under one shared budget. Small cases are then proven complete (degree ≤ 7 with small
   values: Swinnerton-Dyer `x^4-10x^2+1`, `Phi_15`), and larger ones stop honestly.
4. **Multivariate goes beyond §11.2's list.** The Kronecker-image recombination replaces
   "content per variable" and also handles non-homogeneous products. The binomial route is the
   design's "binomial shapes", generalized to monomials U, V.
5. **Rationals are HOLDs with negative exponents internally.** The printer puts them below
   a fraction bar; `factor(360/7)` prints `2^3*3^2*5/7` and its value round-trips.
6. **Approx mode makes factors monic**, which §6.3's example needs.
7. **Cyclotomic factors from `binom` are marked final**, so `x^100-1` is complete without
   searching `Phi_100`.

## Known gaps and TODOs

- **Kronecker is exponential.** Products of two quintics, or of two quartics with
  awkward values, run out of `KBUD`. For example, `(x^5+2x^4-3x+11)(x^5-7x^2+x+13)` comes back
  unfactored, with `COMPLETE` 0. A numeric-root recombination would fix most of these for
  about 35 lines: Aberth or Durand-Kerner complex roots, conjugate-pair subsets,
  `round(lc*prod(x-r))`, then `pdivex`. It can never claim irreducibility, and it was left out
  for size. Zassenhaus is the design's stretch goal.
- **Multivariate without a binomial or homogeneous shape and with an image over `FKMAX`**
  stays unfactored. For example, `(x+y+1)^12` expanded has an image of 169. A multivariate
  square-free step (a Kronecker gcd with `dP/dx`, as `norm.tonode` does) would catch powers.
- **No algebraic relations.** `x^2-2*sqrt(2)*x+2` stays as it is (it would need `t^2 = 2`),
  and the Gaussian content of `i*x+i` is not taken out (Gaussian coefficients are refused).
- `log(x)^2-1` factors through its kernels `ln(x)` and `ln(10)`. The result is correct but
  prints as `-(ln(10)+ln(x))*(ln(10)-ln(x))/ln(10)^2`.
- `4^x-1` stays (the kernel `4^x` is not rewritten as `(2^x)^2`).

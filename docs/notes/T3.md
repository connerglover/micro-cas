# T3: improper integrals, sums and differential equations

This records the implemented T3.1-T3.7 features against DESIGN §11.4. The
implementation has bounded fallbacks described below; the unimplemented DESIGN
items are explicit. T3.8 device validation remains a separate physical-calculator
gate. No timing, heap capacity or download test is claimed here.

## Definite and improper integrals

`defint.g_defint(args, ctx)` handles `integrate(f,x,a,b)`, including reversed and
equal bounds. `defintc.g_points` finds candidate denominator/log/trig
singularities, primitive branch jumps and selected narrow-peak features. Linear
trig roots are enumerated exactly, and general candidates use the solver. The
point cap is 16; exceeding it does not silently truncate the interval.
Integration families are dropped before endpoint-limit and numeric phases.

`g_endpoint` substitutes where possible and otherwise uses one-sided limits.
Each improper piece must converge independently. `DIVG(0)` reports divergence
at infinity and `DIVG(1)` reports a finite singularity; inner divergence propagates
unchanged. `integrate(1/x,x,-1,1)` therefore does not become a principal value.
Without a primitive, bounded numerical quadrature and leading-term divergence
checks are attempted; unclassified cases remain formal.

`defintb.g_quad(f,x,a,b,budget=400)` uses cached tanh-sinh samples, at most six
refinement levels, relative convergence tolerance `2e-8`, and polling every 64
new samples. Finite, half-infinite and whole-line mappings are supported. Exact
results are checked at relative tolerance `1e-6`. Invalid/non-real samples or
exhausted budgets return failure. A result with no sampled evidence is rejected
to avoid presenting a missed narrow peak as zero; quadratic feature splitting
also protects tested peaks above nonzero baselines. The driver shares its nominal
400-sample budget between pieces, with a minimum of 32 per piece; it is not a
strict aggregate 400-call bound when there are many pieces.

## Sums and Riemann sums

`sums.g_sum(args, ctx)` implements `sum(f,k,a,b)`. Finite integer ranges of at most
1,000 terms use an exact, cancellable loop with arena compaction. Larger or
symbolic ranges try closed forms: a triangular discrete-antiderivative system
for polynomials through degree 12, geometric and polynomial-geometric sums, and
verified telescoping candidates with shifts 1 through 4. Unsupported expressions
remain sums. Closed forms are normalized but are not guaranteed to print factored.

`sumsc.g_infinite` supports geometric series; telescoping limits; even zeta values
through ζ(8); alternating harmonic and Leibniz series; exponential generating
series; and the `r^k/k` and `k*r^k` families. Known formulas subtract finite prefixes
and reject an undefined starting term. Pure convergent p-series outside the exact
table use 200 terms plus an Euler-Maclaurin tail and are marked `≈`. A failed
nth-term limit, including oscillation, yields `DIVG(2)`. Recognized divergent
p-series return signed infinity. The lower bound must be an integer from 0
through 1,000 for these infinite strategies.

`g_riemann(args, ctx)` implements left/right/midpoint/trapezoid methods. Right is
the default. Numeric subdivision counts are integers from 1 through 1,000;
symbolic counts build an indexed sum and reuse polynomial summation. Fresh local
indices avoid capture. The exact `x^2` right sum on `[0,1]` is equivalent to
`(n+1)*(2*n+1)/(6*n^2)`, and its limit is `1/3`.

## ODEs and gradients

`ode.g_desolve(args, ctx)` accepts an equation or a list of an equation and initial
conditions, with optional dependent variable: for example
`desolve([y''+4*y=0,y(0)=1,y'(0)=0],y)`. The shared parser preserves derivative
symbols in this context. First-order linear equations use an integrating factor;
separable equations integrate both sides and preserve implicit answers when
inversion is unavailable. Lost zero and recognized quadratic equilibrium
solutions are included. Bernoulli exponent two is handled by the reciprocal
transformation.

Second-order rational constant-coefficient equations use the three characteristic
root cases. A particular solution for a sum of polynomial/exponential/sine/cosine
terms uses the shifted differential-operator recurrence, including simple and
double resonance. Polynomial forcing degree is bounded at six; exponential and
trig slopes must be real rational constants. Initial conditions are solved by
node-valued Gauss-Jordan elimination and substituted back; inconsistent conditions
produce `no solution`, and insufficient conditions retain `C1`/`C2`. At most six
conditions and derivatives through order two are accepted. Each returned family
is checked against the original ODE. Unsupported higher-order, nonlinear and
variable-coefficient cases remain `desolve(...)` calls.

`diff.g_grad(args, ctx)` returns a list of partial derivatives. Explicit variables
preserve the requested order; bare `grad(f)` chooses expression symbols in name
order, excluding function names. It reuses the derivative engine in [T2.md](T2.md).

## Nested integrals and geometric macros

Nested exact integrals evaluate inside out, including bounds depending on outer
variables: `integrate(integrate(x*y,y,0,x),x,0,1)` returns `1/8`.
`polararea(r,t,a,b)` integrates `r^2/2`, `paramarea(x,y,t,a,b)` integrates `y*x'`,
and `arclen(f,x,a,b)` integrates `sqrt(1+f'^2)`. Parametric arc length uses the
five-argument form `arclen(x(t),y(t),t,a,b)` and `sqrt(x'^2+y'^2)`; DESIGN's
six-argument wording does not match its five inputs. These macros reuse the
derivative, simplification and definite-integration machinery.

## Remaining DESIGN differences

- The whole-expression 10×10 mapped Gauss-Legendre fallback for an unresolved
  inner integral is absent. For example, the bounded double Gaussian integral
  `integrate(integrate(exp(-x^2-y^2),y,0,1),x,0,1)` remains unevaluated.
- Failed exact singularity solving has no general numerical root-isolation
  fallback. Improper numerical handling uses transformed quadrature and
  leading-term checks rather than a separate `I(R), I(2R), I(4R)` growth test.
- Generic infinite-series integral/comparison tests and a general 200-term
  tail-integral/averaged-partial-sum fallback are absent; numerical summation is
  restricted to the recognized convergent p-series family.
- Bernoulli reduction handles a consistent real rational exponent other than zero
  or one. It retains implicit integrating-factor equations to preserve branches;
  exponent two keeps the explicit reciprocal form. Zero equilibria are checked
  against the original equation. Separable solutions do not attempt a general
  symbolic inversion after integration.
- Arc length uses ordinary normalization/integration rather than a separate
  `hard_radicand` classifier and interval-sign algorithm.
- The independent Gauss-Legendre cross-check and quadrature aggregate-budget
  difference are recorded above and in [T2.md](T2.md).

## Verification

`tests/test_defint.py` covers exact, improper, nested, degree-mode and numeric
integrals; sentinels and propagation; wrong-primitive rejection; one-piece
quadrature budgets; narrow peaks; and branch-jump capacity. `tests/test_sums.py`
covers exact finite/closed/infinite sums, cancellation, error paths, symbolic
Riemann formulas and their limits. `tests/test_ode.py` checks explicit and implicit
solutions using independent symbolic residuals, resonance, equilibrium branches,
initial conditions and unsupported-input preservation. `tests/test_diff.py`
includes gradient ordering and derivatives under integral signs.

`tests/corpus/t3.tsv` contains 16 end-to-end commands, including improper integrals,
sums, a Riemann limit, nested integration and geometric macros. Mixed sessions
assert that live, needed and pinned loader references are cleared after commands.
Source and bundled validation results belong in the release handover; neither
desktop execution nor the strict depth model substitutes for running CVTEST and
the application on an EVO.

On 2026-10-11, this source-level focused run passed **467 tests** in 117.80 s:

```powershell
.venv/Scripts/python.exe -m pytest tests/test_diff.py tests/test_series.py tests/test_limit.py tests/test_integ12.py tests/test_integ_advanced.py tests/test_integ678.py tests/test_defint.py tests/test_sums.py tests/test_ode.py tests/test_corpus_t2.py tests/test_corpus_t3.py --strict-depth -q
```

The fixture also checks node invariants, arena reset, simplifier verification
flags and internal-error records. This count records this focused run, not the
whole suite or a bundled/device test.

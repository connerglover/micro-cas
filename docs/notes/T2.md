# T2: calculus implementation

This records the implemented T2.1-T2.8 work and its bounded behavior against
DESIGN §11.3. It describes desktop-tested software, not an EVO device acceptance
result. Definite integration crosses the T2/T3 boundary; see [T3.md](T3.md) for
improper integrals and the remaining DESIGN differences.

## Interfaces and algorithms

- `diff.g_deriv(e, x)` is the shared derivative generator. It walks the node DAG
  iteratively, implements product/power/chain rules and elementary real functions,
  differentiates lists, and applies the full Leibniz rule to bounded integrals.
  `g_diff(args, ctx)` handles `diff(f, x, n)` with orders 0 through 20 and simplifies
  between passes. Unknown functions remain symbolic derivatives. Degree mode is
  encoded by the parser; derivatives of `sign`, `floor`, and `ceil` describe their
  values away from discontinuities.
- `series.g_series(f, x, a, n, side=1)` is shared by Taylor expansion and limits.
  It returns `(valuation, coefficient_ids...)`, with coefficients known through
  exponent at least `n`, or `None` when unsupported. A singleton `(cutoff,)` means
  zero only through exponent `cutoff-1`. Finite expansions use `h=x-a`; infinity
  uses `x=1/h` or `x=-1/h`. Integer Laurent series need no side adjustment.
  The iterative fold uses aligned addition, convolution, constant-power Miller
  recurrences, exponential/logarithmic recurrences, and coupled trig/hyperbolic
  recurrences. Inverse trig uses derivative-series integration; variable powers
  use `exp(g*ln(f))`. Cancellation/division retries increase working order up to
  40. `g_taylor` accepts `taylor(f,x=a,n)` and `series(f,x,a,n)`, defaults to order
  5, caps requested order at 20, and appends `BIGO((x-a)^(n+1))`.
- `limit.g_lim(f, x, a, side=0)` returns an exact or approximate node, a `DNE`
  sentinel, or an unevaluated `limit` call. Side is -1/0/1 for left/both/right.
  `g_limit` exposes both `limit(f,x,a,side)` and `limit(f,x=a,side)`. Strategies
  resolve piecewise functions, try substitution and normalization, inspect a
  Laurent leading term, compare exponential/power/log growth, detect oscillation,
  apply bounded L'Hôpital steps, and rewrite indeterminate powers. Two-sided
  limits compare both sides. The final numerical heuristic samples powers of ten
  for k=2..8, checks convergence, extrapolates, and returns a result marked `≈`.
- `integ.g_int(f, x, depth=0)` returns `(F, R)` with the contract `f=F'+R`.
  `g_integrate` preserves an unsolved remainder as an integral; the runner adds
  the display-only `+C`. Linearity, the integration table and bounded substitution
  lead into polynomial/exponential/trig tabular integration, rational partial
  fractions, trig-power reduction, parts, quadratic radicals and final rational
  substitutions. Candidate primitives are checked by differentiating and asking
  the normalizer's zero oracle; a failed check discards the candidate.

## Integration boundaries

`integ.py`/`itab.py` provide the driver, table and substitutions;
`integ2.py` supplies tabular and trig strategies; `integ2b.py` supplies rational
decomposition and `g_partfrac`; `integ3.py` supplies parts and radical moments;
`integ3b.py` supplies `t=tan(u/2)` and exponential substitutions. Real logarithms
use `ln(abs(u))` where required.

Rational decomposition performs polynomial division, rational factorization and
a rational linear solve. Denominator degree is capped at 12, and residual
irreducible factors must have degree at most two. Repeated quadratic factors use
an integration recurrence. Tabular polynomial degree is capped at 12; trig-power
reductions are bounded at 20. Substitution, parts and final substitutions have
logical depth caps 3, 4 and 2. Quadratic-radical integrals use moment recurrences
with numeric constant quadratic coefficients. These are bounded algebraic
strategies, not a complete elementary integrator.

Definite integration separates singularity discovery, antiderivative evaluation,
one-sided endpoint/jump limits and numerical checks. It catches the branch jumps
in `integrate(1/(2+cos(x)),x,0,2*pi)`. Tanh-sinh quadrature handles finite and
transformed infinite intervals, polls during sampling, and returns `≈` only when
its convergence checks pass. An exact candidate that disagrees with its numerical
check is discarded in favor of the numerical value. Unknown numeric functions and
unsupported symbolic bounds remain formal.

## Deliberate limits and DESIGN differences

- Fractional-power Puiseux series, singular logarithmic series such as `ln(x)` at
  zero, and essential singularities are outside the integer Laurent engine.
- L'Hôpital currently allows four rounds, rather than the five described in
  DESIGN; indeterminate-power rewriting has a separate cap of three.
- Constant powers share the Miller recurrence rather than a separate integer
  binary-power implementation. Rational integration uses bounded direct partial
  fractions rather than a general Hermite reduction, and quadratic radicals use
  moment recurrences rather than the specified Ostrogradsky linear system.
- The definite-integral cross-check uses tanh-sinh itself; the independent
  12-point Gauss-Legendre check specified in DESIGN is not yet implemented.
- Numerical limits and quadrature checks are heuristics, not proofs. Reaching a
  bound or failing verification preserves the unevaluated expression.

## Verification

The calculus tests exercise the production parser, command registry, lazy family
loader, trampoline and printer. Derivatives and primitives are checked with an
independent SymPy oracle; partial fractions must reconstruct their input exactly.
Series tests compare coefficients and remainders, including cancellation,
Laurent poles and both infinities. Limit tests cover discontinuities, domain
sides, growth, oscillation, infinity cancellation and approximate-result flags.
Integration tests cover all strategy groups, degree mode, unsupported remainders,
branch-jump correction and rejection of a deliberately wrong primitive.

`tests/corpus/t2.tsv` adds 12 command-level cases and a repeated mixed session.
Tests also require node-arena reset, clean loader lifetimes and no internal-error
records. The exact test command and current result are recorded with T3 below;
strict desktop depth checking is not a measurement of real calculator memory or
interpreter behavior.
